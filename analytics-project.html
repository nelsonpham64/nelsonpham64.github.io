U.S. Respiratory Mortality Trends

Data Analytics Externship (2020-2023)

Analyzing relationships between COVID-19, pneumonia, influenza, and total deaths

Python Pandas Data Analysis Visualization Power BI

Problem Statement

Public health officials need to understand how different respiratory causes of death interact over time to improve preparedness and response planning. This analysis aimed to answer the research question: What relationships exist between COVID-19 deaths, pneumonia deaths, influenza deaths, and total deaths over time in the United States?

  • Mortality Categories: COVID-19, Pneumonia, Influenza, Total Deaths

Methodology

Data Cleaning & Preprocessing

Performed end-to-end data cleaning and preprocessing in Google Colab, standardizing multi-year U.S. climate station data, resolving missing and inconsistent values, and producing an analysis-ready dataset.

Key Data Cleaning Steps

  • Handled missing values and data inconsistencies
  • Standardized date formats across weekly reporting periods
  • Validated state-level geographic data
  • Ensured data type consistency across mortality categories
  • Created derived features for temporal analysis

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

After cleaning and structuring the dataset, I conducted exploratory data analysis to examine patterns in respiratory-related mortality across time. The analysis focused on understanding overall trends, distributions, and fluctuations in reported deaths for different respiratory causes.

Time-series visualizations were used to identify periods of elevated mortality, while distribution plots helped highlight the frequency and magnitude of death spikes across reporting periods.

Key Findings

The analysis revealed important temporal relationships between different respiratory mortality causes:

  • Identified correlation patterns between COVID-19 and pneumonia deaths
  • Observed seasonal trends in influenza-related mortality
  • Documented periods of elevated total mortality during pandemic peaks
  • Highlighted geographic variations in respiratory death patterns across states
